# Ghana feasibility assessment

**Status:** pre-launch assessment for a simulated demonstration
**As of:** 2026-11-13
**Audience:** founders, product, engineering, counsel and prospective regulated partners
**Important:** this is a product-risk assessment, not legal advice or a determination of licence eligibility.

## Executive decision

PeerArena is technically feasible as a simulated-funds product and as a future B2B/regulated-operator technology platform. It is **not launch-ready for real-money use in Ghana**. The product should remain a non-redeemable demo until the legal classification, licence route, enforceability of P2P wagering, payment structure, data rights, AML controls, responsible-gambling controls and security evidence are independently approved.

The least risky commercial path is to validate the challenge UX with synthetic funds, then evaluate a licensed-operator partnership or B2B software model before operating a consumer real-money service directly.

## Regulatory questions that remain open

### Gaming classification and licence route

The Gaming Act, 2006 (Act 721) contains broad concepts of gaming, betting, stake and game of chance. A P2P challenge product can create exposure even where the platform does not set odds or select a winner. Counsel must obtain a written classification of the proposed markets and determine whether the activity is sports betting, remote interactive gaming, another regulated activity, or a technology service operated by a licensee. Do not infer that listing on a licensed-operator page or a partnership alone authorizes PeerArena to operate.

The Gaming Commission's current licensing materials should be checked for the exact applicant type, capital, premises, technical, fit-and-proper, business-plan, market, advertising and reporting requirements that apply at the time of application. Requirements may change.

### Age, identity and jurisdiction

Participation must be limited to eligible adults after age, identity and geographic checks. Public pseudonyms cannot replace secure retention of verified identity evidence. VPN circumvention, false location declarations and unverified private invitations are prohibited by design.

### Payments, safeguarding and ledger

If real money is ever enabled, the platform must use an authorized payment and safeguarding arrangement. The Payment Systems and Services Act, 2019 (Act 987), Bank of Ghana licensing notices and provider contracts must be reviewed for the intended flow. PeerArena must not independently operate a wallet, escrow, money-transfer service or commingled customer-funds account without the necessary authorization. The demo therefore has no payment endpoint and no redeemable balance.

### AML, sanctions and integrity

Gaming is an AML-sensitive sector. A P2P design creates additional exposure to collusion, account farming, matched betting, manipulation, mule activity, rapid movement of value and fabricated outcomes. FIC guidance, applicable AML legislation, sanctions obligations and any gaming-sector reporting duties require specialist implementation before launch.

### Data protection and cybersecurity

Identity, location, device, payment, responsible-gambling and behavioral data require a Ghana Data Protection Act review, lawful processing basis, retention schedule, processor contracts, data-subject rights process and breach response. Cybersecurity obligations, incident notification, vulnerability management and evidence preservation must be mapped to the production architecture.

### Sports data and settlement rights

Every market needs a licensed, reliable source, documented settlement rule, event-completion signal, discrepancy handling and audit record. The business must contract for commercial use and settlement rights; scraping or republishing protected data is not an acceptable shortcut.

### Responsible gambling and marketing

The product must support age gating, limits, cooling-off, self-exclusion, session reminders, statements, complaints and support pathways. Advertising must not imply guaranteed income, financial security, easy money or social pressure. The demo keeps self-exclusion available and visibly labels all funds as fictional.

## Blocking launch gates

All of the following are required before a real-money pilot:

1. Written legal classification and enforceability opinion.
2. Confirmed Gaming Commission licence category, conditions and technical submission path.
3. Approved jurisdiction, age, identity and geo-eligibility rules.
4. Authorized payment/safeguarding structure and provider approval for gaming transactions.
5. Licensed sports-data and result-verification agreements.
6. AML/KYC, sanctions, transaction-monitoring and suspicious-activity procedures.
7. Data protection impact assessment and security/privacy review.
8. Responsible-gambling policy, support referral and self-exclusion operating process.
9. Independent penetration test, threat-model review and ledger/settlement reconciliation evidence.
10. Production incident, dispute, complaints, refund, chargeback and regulatory-reporting procedures.
